BEGINNER'S GUIDE

How to Solve a
3×3 Rubik's Cube

Learn the beginner-friendly layer-by-layer method and solve your first Rubik's Cube step by step.

Start Learning ↓

01 — INTRODUCTION

What is a Rubik's Cube?

The Rubik's Cube is a three-dimensional combination puzzle invented by Hungarian professor Ernő Rubik. A standard 3×3 cube has six faces, each containing nine coloured squares.

The objective is simple: mix up the cube and then rearrange the pieces until each face contains only one colour.

Our goal: Solve the cube layer by layer, starting with the white face and finishing with the yellow face.

02 — CUBE BASICS

Know Your Cube

Before solving the cube, it is important to understand the three types of pieces.

●

Center Pieces

The six center pieces determine the colour of each face. They do not change their position relative to each other.

◆

Edge Pieces

Edge pieces have two colours. A 3×3 cube contains twelve edge pieces.

◆

Corner Pieces

Corner pieces have three colours. There are eight corners on a standard 3×3 cube.

03 — BASIC NOTATION

Understanding Cube Moves

Rubik's Cube algorithms use letters to describe which face should be turned.

RRight
LLeft
UUp
DDown
FFront
BBack
Tip: A letter followed by an apostrophe (') means turn the face counter-clockwise. The number 2 means turn the face twice.

04 — BEGINNER METHOD

Solve the Cube Step by Step

The beginner method solves the cube layer by layer. Follow the steps in order and keep the cube orientation consistent while performing the algorithms.

1

Make the White Cross

Start by creating a white cross around the white center. Each white edge must also match the colour of the center piece on the adjacent side.

There is no single algorithm required for this step. Instead, use simple turns to bring the four white edges into the correct positions.

Goal: A white cross with all four side colours matching their center pieces.
2

Solve the White Corners

Find a white corner and place it below its correct position. Hold the cube so that the corner is at the bottom-right position.

Repeat the following algorithm until the corner is correctly inserted.

R' D' R D

Repeat the same sequence until the white sticker is facing upward and the corner matches both side centres.

3

Solve the Second Layer

Now solve the four edge pieces in the middle layer. First find an edge on the top layer that does not contain yellow.

Match the front colour of the edge with its corresponding center. Then decide whether the edge needs to move to the right or left.

Move the edge to the right:

U R U' R' U' F' U F

Move the edge to the left:

U' L' U L U F U' F'
4

Create the Yellow Cross

Turn the cube so that the yellow center is on top. The goal is to create a yellow cross on the top face.

Depending on whether you see a dot, an L-shape, or a straight line, the algorithm may need to be performed more than once.

F R U R' U' F'
Remember: At this stage, only the yellow cross matters. The yellow corners do not need to be solved yet.
5

Position the Yellow Edges

Now match the four yellow edge pieces with the centre colours on the four side faces.

Hold the two matching yellow edges at the back and right. If the matching edges are opposite each other, hold the cube in any orientation and perform the algorithm.

R U R' U R U2 R' U

You may need to perform the algorithm more than once. Rotate the top layer between attempts to set up the correct position.

until all four yellow edges match their centres.

6

Position the Yellow Corners

Now place each yellow corner into its correct position. The yellow stickers do not need to face upward yet.

Find a corner that is already in the correct position and hold it at the front-right-top position.

U R U' L' U R' U' L

Repeat the algorithm as necessary until all four yellow corners are in their correct positions.

7

Orient the Yellow Corners

This is the final step. Keep the cube in the same orientation and place an unsolved yellow corner at the front-right-top position.

R' D' R D

Repeat the sequence until the yellow sticker of that corner faces upward. Then turn only the top layer to bring the next unsolved corner into the same position.

Important: The cube may look scrambled while doing this step. Do not panic and do not rotate the entire cube. Continue the sequence and the cube will eventually solve itself.

05 CONCLUSION

Keep Practicing!

Solving a Rubik's Cube for the first time can seem difficult, but the process becomes much easier once the basic moves and algorithms are familiar.

Practice each step separately and eventually you will be able to solve the complete cube without looking at the guide.
